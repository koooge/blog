In September 2026, I passed course D of SFI (Svenska för invandrare, or Swedish for immigrants) and graduated from SFI.
I moved to Sweden in 2019, so it has been roughly seven years. That doesn't mean I was studying Swedish seriously from day one, though. Here's a look back at learning Swedish alongside work and parenting.

## English came first
I wasn't attending SFI all those years. At first, I focused on English and picked up a little Swedish through an app. I had never studied abroad, and this was my first time living overseas. My TOEIC score was just under 800 at the time. My English still needed work, so I figured I'd start there. Funny enough, even though I wasn't confident in my English, seeing it around town felt reassuring.
I did use Duolingo for Swedish from shortly after moving. It was useful for learning basic vocabulary and grammar.

## Starting SFI
As the pandemic eased, I started thinking about studying Swedish for everyday life. SFI has four courses, A through D. Your starting point depends on your educational background and knowledge of Swedish, and D is the final course.
When a remote course became available, I started studying seriously in course C. Classes were on Microsoft Teams twice a week, about an hour and a half each. I attended while working, and we also had time to talk with classmates.

We had a child after I started SFI, so I continued studying alongside work and parenting. People living in Sweden generally speak good English, but some only speak Swedish. At förskola (preschool), in particular, many teachers don't speak English. These days, I do my best to talk with them in my still-halting Swedish.

## Listening is hard
Of the four skills—reading, listening, writing, and speaking—listening was the hardest for me. I struggled with it in the exams too.
Swedish has nine vowel letters: a, e, i, o, u, y, å, ä, and ö. Japanese has five vowels, and many of the Swedish sounds seem similar to my ears. Telling them apart was difficult. Short words are especially hard to distinguish.
Around the time I started course C, I began practicing listening with SR's "Radio Sweden på lätt svenska" (radio news in easy Swedish) and "SVT Nyheter" (SVT news). I'd sit at my desk, watch or listen online, and look up unfamiliar words in a dictionary. Even with that practice, listening remained difficult through the end of the course.
Rather than leaving the news on in the background, I studied at my desk and worked through the content. It was listening practice, but looking up words and checking the text meant reading was part of it too.

## I used AI too
AI was developing rapidly while I was studying, so I also used ChatGPT and Gemini. I'd ask them to translate news articles and other texts into English and Japanese to check the meaning and sentence structure.
Because Swedish has similarities to English, I sometimes found the English translation more useful for understanding the structure. The English I'd prioritized after moving here came in handy for learning Swedish too.

## Graduating after about two years
I passed the course C exam in May 2025 and course D in September 2026. About two years after getting serious about studying, I finally graduated from SFI, alongside work and parenting.
Looking back, I gradually added more tools: Duolingo, classes, news, and AI. Learning vocabulary and grammar, talking with classmates, and listening to the news and looking things up all gave me different ways to practice Swedish.

## Next up: SVA grund
These days, I try to use Swedish for simple interactions, like ordering at a shop. Little by little, I'm using what I've studied in everyday life.
Next, I plan to move on to SVA grund (basic-level Swedish as a second language). I'll keep studying and practicing Swedish in daily life.
